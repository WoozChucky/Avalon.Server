# Trading (server design)

Server-side rules for face-to-face trading (#1175). The client contract is [Trade protocol](trade-protocol.md).

## Trading

Two players standing next to each other swap items and gold directly: each puts up to 15 Bag items and an amount of gold into a shared window, both see both offers, both confirm, and the swap happens all at once or not at all. Nothing leaves its owner until the trade's save has committed, so a cancel, a crash or a disconnect never loses or duplicates an item or gold. It is the only path that moves items or gold between two characters.

- **`TradeService`** (`Avalon.World/Trading`, a DI singleton next to `PartyService`, World-side; nothing about trading is on `Avalon.World.Public`, since the modding API may not move items between characters) holds every request and open trade **in memory only**: a restart drops them all, and a trade lives on one world server. **Tick thread only, every method**: the seven handlers, `World.TransferPlayer` (`InstanceChanged`), `World.LeaveWorldAsync` (`CharacterOffline`), the NPC window paths in `DialogueChooseHandler` (`NpcWindowOpening`) and `World.Update` (`Tick`, after the party tick and before the instances, contained with its own `ThrottledErrorLog`, "The trade tick") all call it on the tick, and every mutator asserts `TickThreadGuard.AssertOnTick("TradeService.<Method>")`. No timers: every deadline is read from the container's `TimeProvider`. With no request and no trade, `Tick` returns after reading two counts and allocates nothing; an open trade costs its automatic-cancel checks and allocates nothing either (`TradeBenchmarks`, [benchmarks](benchmarks.md)). `World.LoadAsync` hands it the economy and the item templates (`Attach(ICharacterEconomy, Data.FindItemTemplate)`), resolved there rather than injected because the economy depends on the `World`. A `Trade` (`Id`, a `uint` numbered from 1 per run; `First`, the sender of the accepted request, and `Second`, each a `TradeSide`; `State`, `Open` or `Completing`; `Revision`; `RecordId`) is changed only by the service. A character holds at most one request, sent or received, and is in at most one trade.
- **Requests** (a `TradeResult` each; refusals in the order they are checked). `Request(sender, target)`, the target named by character id (a right-click on a player, never a name): `NotFound` (the sender is not online), `Dead`, `InCombat`, `NpcWindowOpen` (a shop or the bank open, `OpenShopNpc` or `OpenBankNpc`), `Busy` (the sender is trading or holds a request), `TooFast` (the budget, below), `Self`, `NotFound` (the target is not online in the sender's instance), `TargetDead`, `TargetInCombat`, `TooFar` (beyond `TradeRules.RequestRange`, 5 m), `TargetBusy` (the target is trading or holds a request). The target's NPC window is not checked here: accepting is refused instead. A request that passes sends the target `SMSG_TRADE_REQUEST` and expires after `Game:TradeRequestTimeoutSeconds` (30). **Ignore (#723):** a request to a target that ignores the sender (`Ignoring.Hides`, checked only after every refusal) is answered `Ok` exactly as a sent one and never reaches the target; it occupies the sender's one request but not the target's, so the target can still send and receive others, and it expires for the sender alone (`Expired`), so nothing discloses the ignore. **Request budget:** a limit on request spam, `CharacterRateBudget` (the chat and party invite limits' mechanism) with `Game:TradeRequestsPerMinute` requests in any sliding 60 s (default 30 in `appsettings.json`, not in C#; 0 or below turns it off). It is checked after `Busy` and before the target is looked up, and every request that gets that far spends one, whatever the lookup finds; past it the request is answered `TooFast` and the service looks nothing up (the handler still names the target when it is online, as on every answer to a request). The budget is forgotten when the sender goes offline, as the party invite budget is.
- **Answering a request.** `Respond(target, accept)`: `NoRequest` unless the character is the target of a request it can see. A decline ends the request and tells the sender `Declined`. An accept is refused `NotFound` (the accepter is not online), `Dead`, `InCombat`, `NpcWindowOpen`, `NotFound` (the sender is not online in the accepter's instance), `TargetDead`, `TargetInCombat`, `TargetNpcWindowOpen` (41, the sender has a shop or the bank open), `TooFar` (beyond `TradeRules.KeepRange`, the 6 m dialogue leash, `NpcInteraction.LeashRange`: an accept is judged at the range that keeps an open trade, so a step after the request does not refuse it). A refused accept leaves the request open until it expires. An accept that passes ends the request, opens the trade, sends both `SMSG_TRADE_OPENED` and then the first state. An expiry tells the sender `Expired` (naming the target) and the target `Expired` (naming the sender), unless the request was hidden. A character going offline ends every request it sent or holds, the other side told `Expired`; a character changing instance ends them too, both sides told `Expired`.
- **The window.** Each side has 15 item slots (`TradeRules.MaxItems`) and one gold amount, empty and 0 at the start. Every edit is refused `NotTrading` without a trade and `Completing` once the trade is completing. `OfferItem(bagSlot, tradeSlot)` puts the whole stack in a Bag slot into a trade slot: `InvalidSlot` (a trade slot past 14 or a Bag slot the Bag lacks), `ItemNotFound` (the Bag slot is empty), `AlreadyOffered` (that instance is in the trade), `TradeSlotTaken` (the trade slot is occupied), `NotTradeable` (`TradeRules.IsTradeable`: the template has `ItemTemplateFlags.NoTrade` (1024) or `QuestItem` (2048), or is gone). The item stays in the Bag and is locked (below). Equipment and the Bank are never offered, and a part of a stack never is: split it in the Bag first. `RemoveItem(tradeSlot)`: `InvalidSlot`, `ItemNotFound` (the trade slot is empty); the item is unlocked. `SetGold(gold)`: `NotEnoughGold` above the balance; the amount is held (below) from the moment it is set. **Every change** to either offer clears both confirmations, moves `Revision` on by one, and sends both players the whole trade (`SMSG_TRADE_STATE`); a change that changes nothing (the gold already set) sends nothing and clears nothing. `Confirm(revision)` approves the trade as it stands at that revision: one for an older revision is refused `StaleOffer`, and the confirmer is sent the trade as it now stands; a confirm already given is `Ok` and changes nothing; otherwise the side is marked confirmed and both are sent the state. When both have confirmed the current revision the trade completes (below). `Cancel` closes the trade for both, `Cancelled` to the canceller and `CancelledByPartner` to the other; nothing moves. The state packet reuses the service's own two side DTOs, their two item lists and one item entry per trade slot of each side (as the party roster does, #973): each recipient's packet is encoded before the next is built, so a state allocates only its two packets.
- **Automatic cancel.** `Tick` closes each open trade with the first of these that holds, the same reason to both: `Left` (a trader's connection no longer holds its character, or the two are in different instances), `Died`, `EnteredCombat`, `NpcWindowOpened`, `OutOfRange` (beyond `KeepRange`, 6 m). The hooks close it at once: `InstanceChanged` (from `World.TransferPlayer`, so every portal entry, respawn and countdown move) with `Left` to both; `CharacterOffline` (from `World.LeaveWorldAsync`: logout, disconnect, kick, Change Character) with `Left` to the partner only; `NpcWindowOpening` (called before `OpenBankNpc` or `OpenShopNpc` is set) with `NpcWindowOpened` to both, so a trade and an NPC window never overlap. A completing trade is closed by none of these: its save decides. Closing a trade, for any reason, releases every lock and reservation on both sides.

## Locks and reservations

- **`InventoryLocks`** (`Avalon.World/Inventory`, one per character, `CharacterEntity.Locks`, World-side, never saved, tick thread only) holds what the character has offered: the locked item instances, which stay in its Bag, and the held gold (`HeldGold`), which stays in its balance; what can be spent is `Spendable(balance)`, the balance less the held gold. While the trade completes it also holds what the character is about to receive: the Bag slots the incoming items go to (`Reserve`, one bit per slot), the incoming copies of each template (`ExpectIncoming`, `IncomingCount`) and the incoming gold (`IncomingGold`). `Clear` drops all of it. Every check asks `Any` first (or is a check that does: `IsLocked` short-circuits on it), one bool read, so with nothing offered or reserved no path changes and nothing allocates. The lists are made on first use, sized for a full trade, and kept.
- **Every path that honours them.** A locked instance cannot be moved, split, merged into, swapped with, banked, equipped, destroyed, sold, used or consumed; adds never fill its stack, and removals by template never see it. A reserved slot is taken for every add but the trade's own, the incoming copies count toward a Unique template's one copy, and the incoming gold counts toward `Game:MaxGold`. So an add during the completing window (loot, a quest reward, a purchase) may be refused as bag full, gold cap or unique.
  - `InventoryMove.Decide` (`CMSG_ITEM_MOVE`, so a drag into Equipment or the Bank too): a move, split, merge or swap whose source or target item is locked, or whose target is a reserved Bag slot, is `ItemRequestResult.Locked` (14, appended), checked after `NotFound` and before the count. `InventoryMove.DecideDestroy` (`CMSG_ITEM_DESTROY`): `Locked`.
  - `IInventoryService` (`CharacterInventoryService`): `TryRemove(instance)` is `InventoryRemoveResult.Locked`; `TryRemove(template)` takes only unlocked stacks; `TryAdd` and `CanAdd` neither fill a locked stack nor use or count a reserved slot, and count `IncomingCount` toward Unique; `TryAddInstance` (buyback) takes the lowest slot neither occupied nor reserved and counts the incoming copies. `TryAddInstanceAt(item, bagSlot)` is the trade's own add: only an occupied slot refuses it, and only what the character holds counts toward Unique.
  - `IWallet` (`CharacterWallet`): `TrySpend` spends only the spendable balance (`NotEnoughGold`); `TryAddGold` counts `IncomingGold` as already in the balance (`InventoryLocks.CanReceiveGold`, the same no-wrap rule).
  - `ItemUseService`: a Bag item that is locked is `ItemUseResult.Locked` (18, appended), asked before the template; a cast bar whose item became locked ends `Interrupted` at completion; taking a worn item off (`ItemEquip`) skips reserved slots (`TargetFull` when none is left).
  - `VendorRules`: buy and buyback spend only the spendable balance (`NotEnoughGold`); a buy's cost items come only from unlocked Bag stacks (`MissingItems`); room skips locked partial stacks and reserved slots (`InventoryFull`); Unique counts the incoming copies (`UniqueAlreadyOwned`); a sell of a locked item is `VendorResult.Locked` (14, appended), and its gold cap counts the incoming gold (`GoldCapReached`).
  - Quests (`QuestTurnInRules`, `QuestService`): a turn-in counts only unlocked Collect stacks (a locked one leaves it `NotReady`), keeps a locked stack as taken room and does not fill a locked partial stack (`BagFull`), skips reserved slots, and counts the incoming gold toward the cap (`GoldCap`); turn-in and abandon both leave a locked stack where it is (`RemoveQuestItems`).
  - Loot pickup and item scripts add through `TryAdd` and `CanAdd`, so they meet the same rules.

## Completion: save first, then swap

- **Both confirmed** (`TradeService.Completion.cs`). The player checks run first, the automatic cancel's own (`Left`, `Died`, `EnteredCombat`, `NpcWindowOpened`, `OutOfRange`): a failure closes the trade with that reason, as the automatic cancel would. Then the completion rules, `TradeRules.Plan` (pure, the first side first at each step, the first failure answering): every offered item still in its Bag slot with the same instance and count, and tradeable (`OfferInvalid`); each giver holds the gold offered (`OfferInvalid`); each receiver's Bag has a free slot per incoming item, counting the slots its own outgoing items free (incoming items are placed whole, never merged; else `BagFull`); no receiver ends up owning two of a Unique item, across Equipment, Bag and Bank once its outgoing items leave (`UniqueAlreadyOwned`); no receiver's balance passes `Game:MaxGold` (`GoldCapReached`). Destination slots are the lowest free ones, ascending, given to the incoming items in offer order. The plan carries each item as the Bag holds it now, not the copy taken when it was offered, so the save and both memories name the same item state. A failure keeps the window open: the failing side is sent the result, the partner its `Partner` form (`PartnerBagFull`, `PartnerGoldCapReached`, `PartnerUniqueAlreadyOwned`; `OfferInvalid` as it is) naming the failing player, and the trade is changed (both confirmations cleared, the revision moved on, the state sent). Only player checks close the trade; item, room, gold and Unique failures keep it.
- **Completing.** The plan passed: the trade turns `Completing`, each receiver's destination slots are reserved and its incoming copies and gold expected (`ExpectIncoming`), the trade record is built (`RecordId`, below), and the paired save is started: `ICharacterSaver.SaveTrade([(connection, character, side plan) × 2], record)`. Both are sent the state with `Completing` set. Nothing in memory has moved: the offered items stay locked and the gold held until the outcome. `SaveTrade` throws only before it queues anything (nothing written, nothing held): the trade closes with `Error` to both. The save and the record lookups run off the tick; their continuations only enqueue what they learned (`ConcurrentQueue`), and `Tick` applies it.
- **The paired save** (`CharacterSaver.SaveTrade`). Each character is snapshotted on the tick (`CharacterSaveSnapshot.Take`) and its batch rewritten as it will be once its side is applied (`TradeSaveBatch.Apply`, which never touches the live row or containers): a trade changes an item's owner (#1179), so an outgoing item is never deleted: its Bag slot is deleted unless an incoming item lands there, and whatever the snapshot held for it (its row, its modifier write) is dropped; each incoming item is upserted as this character's (an update of `CharacterId` when the row exists, an insert when it was never saved) with a slot row at its destination and its whole modifier set, copied on the tick from the plan's `CarriedItem`; the row a copy with the gold given and received. Both batches and the record go in **one transaction** (`ICharacterSaveRepository.WriteTradeAsync`, every delete before any upsert), chained behind every earlier save of either character. Marks are acknowledged on a commit only. The fence (`GuardAsync`) refuses any batch naming an item another character outside the batch owns, which is why the item can move only in one transaction holding both characters.
- **The hold.** On the same tick call as the enqueue, both characters are held (a `SaveHold` each) until `ReleaseHold`. While held, `Save` and `SaveOnDespawnAsync` take **no snapshot**: each is queued on the hold, in the order asked for, and snapshotted and chained only at the release, its caller's task completing with that save's result (false should that save ever fault, which by contract it never does, so a held logout never hangs). A multi-character `Save` naming a held character, or a second `SaveTrade` for one, throws before queuing anything. `WhenIdle(id)` of a held character completes only once the hold is released and every save it kept waiting has finished; `WhenAllIdle` waits for every hold too. **Why chain order alone could not do it:** the character row, gold included, is written whole on every save, so a save snapshotted before the swap writes the pre-trade row back wherever it sits in the chain: ordered after the paired save, as the chain orders it, it would land after the commit and hand back the gold just paid. What makes a save safe is when its snapshot is taken, not where it is written, so a held save is snapshotted only once the swap is applied. The same hold lets leaving, a transfer and Change Character apply the outcome before their save without `LeaveWorldAsync` ever waiting: it must queue its save before its first await, and does.
- **Outcomes** (`SaveOutcome`): `Committed`, the swap; `Refused` (nothing was written), the trade closes with `SaveFailed` to each side still online, every lock and reservation released, nothing moved; `Unknown` (the commit may have landed), the record is looked up (below) and the trade stays completing, with its locks, reservations and holds, until it is found (the swap) or found absent (`SaveFailed`). Every path after a `SaveTrade` that did not throw releases both holds, each even if the other's release throws, but one: a trade still unsettled at shutdown (below). Applying one outcome that throws (a release, after the trade has closed) is logged at Error and never keeps the other outcomes from being applied, on the tick or in the shutdown settle.
- **The swap**, on the tick, through `IInventoryService` and `IWallet` only: both sides' locks cleared; every offered item taken out of each giver (`TakeOut`, the whole stack, only when still at its slot with the same instance and count) and handed over (`SaveState.ItemHandedOver`); each receiver's incoming items added at their planned slots (`TryAddInstanceAt`); each side's gold spent (`TrySpend`), then received (`TryAddGold`). A refusal there is a programmer error, logged at Critical; what a giver did not lose its receiver does not gain (nothing is created on the other side), so memory never holds more than the database, and memory differs from it until the next login. An item `TakeOut` skipped stays with the giver in memory, unmarked as handed over, while the database gives it to the receiver: the giver's next change to it marks an item another character owns, and the fence then refuses every save of the giver until logout (the Critical line says so). Then both holds are released, so every held save is snapshotted after the swap, and the trade closes with `Ok` to each side still online. The inventory and gold changes reach both clients in the tick's `SMSG_INVENTORY_UPDATE`, after `SMSG_TRADE_CLOSED`. The receiver's new items carry `New` marks, so its next save upserts them again, idempotently.
- **`ItemHandedOver` and the fence.** Taking the item out marks it `Removed` in the giver's `SaveState`. The paired save already wrote it, with its modifiers, as the receiver's, and the fence refuses any later save of the giver that still names an item another character owns, so the mark would fail every save of the giver until logout. `ItemHandedOver` forgets its whole mark instead, the modifiers flag with it. The giver's `TakeOut` hands the item's modifiers (a `CarriedItem`) to the receiver's `TryAddInstanceAt`, which puts them in the receiver's `ItemModifierStore` without marking them: the paired save wrote them.
- **Leaving, transfer and Change Character while completing.** `CharacterOffline` (run in `World.LeaveWorldAsync` while the connection still holds the character, **before** the party's offline call, which takes the character off `OnlineCharacters`, the entry the trade service uses to tell a stale connection's leave apart, and before the despawn save is queued) does not cancel a completing trade: it marks the side offline. The despawn save is held; on the outcome the swap (or nothing) is applied to the entity the trade still references, and the release snapshots the despawn save with it applied, so it writes the committed result. The side offline is told nothing; its partner hears `Ok` or `SaveFailed`. A transfer (`InstanceChanged`) leaves a completing trade to its outcome; the save a map entry makes (`EnterMapHandler`, `MapTeleport`) is held the same way, and the swap applies wherever the two now stand. Change Character is a logout: its `Left` answer is sent only once the logout save committed, so it arrives after the trade's outcome. A relog waits on `WhenIdle`, which a hold extends, bounded as always by `CharacterSelectHandler.SaveWaitLimit` (5 s; past it the select is cancelled and the client may select again). Opening an NPC window while completing closes nothing; the locks and reservations keep the shop and the bank off what the trade planned.
- **Shutdown** (`WorldServer.OnStoppingAsync`). After the tick is joined, the closes run and every despawn starts, so every other character's despawn save is queued and writing before trading waits for anything; a trader's despawn save waits on its hold. Then `TradeService.SettleAsync(SaveDrainLimit, stoppingToken)` runs inside the shutdown's one save window (`SaveDrainLimit`, 20 s, from the moment every despawn has started), alongside the saves already writing: it waits for each completing trade's save, looks up each unknown outcome's record with the same backoff, and applies each outcome as `Tick` does (it asserts the tick thread, free there since the tick has unbound the guard), waking on whatever comes first (a save or lookup finishing, a retry falling due, the limit). It returns whether every completing trade settled. With one unsettled, the despawn wait and the final `WaitForSavesAsync` get only what is left of the window, since that trade's held saves never drain; with every trade settled they are as before. A tick that outlived its 5 s join leaves the settle skipped, logged at Error (the tick could still be applying completions itself), and counts as unsettled.
- **Logs.** A completed swap writes one Information line, the audit line: `Trade {TradeId} completed: character {First} gave [{FirstItems}] and {FirstGold} gold, character {Second} gave [{SecondItems}] and {SecondGold} gold (record {RecordId})`, each item as `instance:template x count`. A swap with a refused step writes the same plan at Error instead, as "completed with errors (see the Critical lines above)", so the audit line never claims a clean swap that was not. An unknown commit logs a Warning, each failed lookup a Warning with its attempt, a refused save a Warning ("was not saved; nothing moved"), a swap refusal a Critical, an outcome that failed to apply an Error, and an unsettled trade at shutdown an Error. Opening and closing are Debug, through generated `[LoggerMessage]` methods, which check the level before formatting, so with Debug off they cost a level check and allocate nothing.

## The trade record

- **Tables** (Character DB, migration `AddTradeRecords`; `TradeRecord` and `TradeRecordItem` in `Avalon.Domain/Characters`). `TradeRecords`: `Id` (`uuid`, `Guid.CreateVersion7()` from the server, never generated by the database), `FirstCharacterId` and `SecondCharacterId` (`bigint`; first is the sender of the accepted request), `FirstGold` and `SecondGold` (`numeric(20,0)`, a `ulong`: the gold each gave), `CompletedAt` (`timestamp with time zone`, UTC: when both confirmed and the save started, not the commit time). Indexes `IX_TradeRecords_FirstCharacterId` and `IX_TradeRecords_SecondCharacterId`. `TradeRecordItems`, one row per item a side gave: `TradeId` (`uuid`, foreign key to `TradeRecords`, cascade), `ItemInstanceId` (`uuid`), `GiverCharacterId` (`bigint`; the receiver is the record's other character), `TemplateId` (`numeric(20,0)`), `Count` (`bigint`, a `uint`), keyed by `(TradeId, ItemInstanceId)` and indexed on `ItemInstanceId` (`IX_TradeRecordItems_ItemInstanceId`).
- **No foreign key to characters, items or templates**: the record is the permanent trade history and outlives a deleted character (whose hard delete cascades its items), an item used up or destroyed, and a template a reload removed; the templates live in another database anyway. **Child rows**, not a JSON or array column, so the history is queryable by character and by item instance with plain indexes on Postgres and on the SQLite the tests use.
- **Written in the paired save's transaction** (`WriteTradeAsync` adds the record, with its items, before the one `SaveChanges`), so the record exists exactly when the swap committed.
- **Three outcomes and where `SaveOutcomeUnknownException` comes from.** `CharacterSaveRepository.WriteAsync` and `WriteTradeAsync` set a flag on the work's last line, after the one `SaveChanges` and the lease deadline check; the transaction runner's only step after the work is the commit (and disposing the transaction and context). An exception with the flag set came from the commit or after it, and is thrown as `SaveOutcomeUnknownException` (the original inside): the write may or may not have committed. Any other exception (a fence or lease refusal, `GameplayWriteRejectedException`, a constraint, a connection that never opened) came before the commit, rolled the transaction back, and means nothing was written. `CharacterSaver` maps them to `Committed`, `Unknown` and `Refused` for a trade; any other save treats an unknown commit as failed, keeps its marks, and the next save writes the same idempotent values again.
- **The lookup.** `ICharacterSaver.TradeCommittedAsync(recordId, firstAccount, secondAccount)` runs on the thread pool, not chained behind any save, under `CharacterSaver.TradeLookupDeadline` (5 s); `ICharacterSaveRepository.TradeRecordExistsAsync` takes, in its own transaction, both accounts' gameplay fence locks (`GameplayFenceRepository.LockAsync`, the lower account id first, the order every save takes them in, so the two never deadlock), and only then reads the record. **Why "absent" is final:** a paired write holds those locks from its fence check until its transaction ends, so the read waits for a write still committing, and on Postgres (Read Committed) a statement after the wait sees what that write committed; the lookup starts only after the save's outcome came back, and nothing else ever inserts that record, so absent after the locks means it never will be there. A lookup that fails or runs past the deadline (a connection idle in its transaction can hold the locks indefinitely) faults, and is retried, never guessed: after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 s, then every 30 s, for as long as the server runs.
- **Reading the history.** A trader's trades, by character id:

  ```sql
  SELECT * FROM "TradeRecords"
  WHERE "FirstCharacterId" = 42 OR "SecondCharacterId" = 42
  ORDER BY "CompletedAt";
  ```

  An item's history, by instance id (each row is one hand-over: the giver, and the record's other character as the receiver):

  ```sql
  SELECT r."Id", r."CompletedAt", i."GiverCharacterId", r."FirstCharacterId", r."SecondCharacterId", i."TemplateId", i."Count"
  FROM "TradeRecordItems" AS i JOIN "TradeRecords" AS r ON r."Id" = i."TradeId"
  WHERE i."ItemInstanceId" = '0192f0c4-0000-7000-8000-000000000000'
  ORDER BY r."CompletedAt";
  ```

  The record id is in the audit line, and in the Warning and Error lines of an unknown or unsettled trade.
- **Known limit.** At shutdown, a trade still unsettled when `SaveDrainLimit` runs out (its save or its lookup never answered) keeps its holds: neither trader's despawn save, nor any other save of theirs asked for since the paired save, is written, so nothing pre-trade is written over a trade that may have committed, and nothing is duplicated. Their changes since the paired save's snapshot are lost. The Error line names the trade, both characters and the record id: an operator finds the record present (the trade committed; the database holds both post-trade states) or absent (it did not; the database holds both as before).

## Protocol and settings

- **Protocol.** Seven requests, each with a `MapSessionFilter` entry and a handler in `Avalon.World/Handlers`: `CMSG_TRADE_REQUEST` (0x20D0, `TargetCharacterId`), `CMSG_TRADE_RESPOND` (0x20D1, `Accept`), `CMSG_TRADE_OFFER_ITEM` (0x20D2, `BagSlot`, `TradeSlot`), `CMSG_TRADE_REMOVE_ITEM` (0x20D3, `TradeSlot`), `CMSG_TRADE_SET_GOLD` (0x20D4, `Gold`), `CMSG_TRADE_CONFIRM` (0x20D5, `Revision`), `CMSG_TRADE_CANCEL` (0x20D6). Each runs through `TradeReplies.Handle` (the service and the packet handed through as state to a static lambda, as for parties, so a request allocates nothing of its own) and gets **exactly one** `SMSG_TRADE_RESULT` (0x30E4: `TradeResult`, append-only, and the other player's name where known: the target's on a request when it is online), sent after anything the request sent itself; a request that throws is logged at Error and answered `Error` (40); a connection with no character gets nothing. Server to client also `SMSG_TRADE_REQUEST` (0x30E0), `SMSG_TRADE_OPENED` (0x30E1), `SMSG_TRADE_STATE` (0x30E2) and `SMSG_TRADE_CLOSED` (0x30E3). [Trade protocol](trade-protocol.md) has every field and value.
- **Settings** (`GameConfiguration`, range-validated at startup): `TradeRequestTimeoutSeconds` 30 (1-3600), `TradeRequestsPerMinute` 30 (in `appsettings.json`; 0 or below off). The gold cap is `Game:MaxGold`. Fixed in code: 15 item slots a side (`TradeRules.MaxItems`), the request range 5 m (`RequestRange`), the keep range 6 m (`KeepRange`), the record lookup's deadline (`CharacterSaver.TradeLookupDeadline`, 5 s) and the shutdown's `WorldServer.SaveDrainLimit` (20 s).
